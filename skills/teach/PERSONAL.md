## Lesson presentation

- Teach at the level of a first-year IT undergraduate. Use plain language and define each piece of jargon before relying on it. A technical term that is the lesson's subject may be introduced by name first, but explain it immediately.
- Make every lesson easy to skim and print. Use clear headings, short paragraphs, lists, readable spacing, and print-friendly styling.
- Ground explanations in concrete examples. Add visual diagrams when they clarify a structure, process, relationship, or sequence. Render lesson diagrams with an HTML `<canvas>` that displays correctly in Chrome and remains clear when printed.
- After every lesson, update the workspace's canonical `GLOSSARY.md` with terms the user has demonstrated they understand. Then regenerate the glossary-derived flashcard deck, using each term and its canonical definition as a card. Use the Leitner system to schedule cards and move them between review boxes based on the user's recall.
- Use commas, periods, parentheses, or short hyphens as separators. Do not use em dashes as text separators.
